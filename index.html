About

I am a theoretical physicist working at the interface of quantum matter, quantum information, and mathematical physics.

My research asks how symmetry, locality, and interactions constrain quantum states and Hamiltonians. Current interests include long-range entanglement, quantum circuit lower bounds and state preparation, quantum many-body scars, quantum anomalies, topological semimetals, chiral fermions, crystalline defects, and quantum simulation.

I completed my Burke Postdoctoral Fellowship at the California Institute of Technology at the end of August 2026 and began a postdoctoral appointment at the University of Colorado Boulder on September 1, 2026. I will join the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Tufts University as an Assistant Professor in September 2027.

Research

  • Symmetry, locality, and entanglement. Constraints on quantum states imposed by translation symmetry, internal symmetries, anomalies, and finite-depth quantum circuits.
  • Quantum many-body states and Hamiltonians. Parent Hamiltonians, quantum many-body scars, special invariant subspaces, W states, and the relation between local dynamics and global structure.
  • Topological semimetals and lattice fermions. Dirac and Weyl systems, chiral symmetries, crystalline responses and defects, interaction effects, and anomaly-based no-go theorems.
  • Quantum state preparation and simulation. Circuit lower bounds, efficient preparation protocols, and structural questions at the boundary of condensed matter theory and quantum information.
